## What it measures

The price/earnings ratio says how many dollars the market pays for one
dollar of a company's annual earnings. Trailing P/E uses the last twelve
months' actual earnings; forward P/E uses analysts' estimate of the next
twelve. A higher number says the market is paying more per dollar of
(trailing, or expected) earnings than a lower one — growth expectations,
scarcity, or simple optimism can all push it up, and none of those are told
apart here.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` resolves the fund's holding list; `fundamentals` reads each
holding's `trailingPE` and `forwardPE` straight from yfinance's own `.info`
(`measurements/inputs/fundamentals.py`), live, with no database path at all
— these move with every price tick (trailing) or every estimate revision
(forward), too often for a once-a-day sync to be worth adding.

## How it is computed

Both columns are read as-is, rounded to two decimal places:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{Trailing P/E} = \frac{\text{Price}}{\text{Trailing 12M EPS}}, \quad \text{Forward P/E} = \frac{\text{Price}}{\text{Estimated next 12M EPS}}`} />

## How it reads

There is no universal "good" P/E — it only means something next to a peer,
a sector average, or the same company's own history. A forward P/E well
below the trailing one says the market expects earnings to grow; well above
says it expects them to shrink.

## Caveats

<Warning>
A company with negative or near-zero trailing earnings has no meaningful
trailing P/E and Yahoo reports none — shown as a dash here, never a
negative or wildly large number standing in for "loss-making."
</Warning>

Forward P/E rests on analyst estimates, which are opinions, not facts, and
can be stale or thin for a less-covered company.
