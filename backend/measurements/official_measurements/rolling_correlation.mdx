title: Correlation to Fund (Rolling)
summary: How a holding's correlation to its fund has moved over the window, not just what it averaged to.
---

## What it measures

An ordinary correlation is one number for the whole window, and an average
hides its own history. A holding whose correlation to the fund climbed from
0.3 to 0.9 and one that fell from 0.9 to 0.3 can both average 0.6 — opposite
findings wearing the same figure. This column shows the path instead: the
holding's correlation to its fund's own weighted return, read separately
over consecutive 30-return blocks across the window rather than once across
all of it.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for each ticker's weight, `price_frame` for closes, `fund_index`
for the fund's own weighted-return series — the same benchmark Beta/R²/
Idiosyncratic Volatility, Tail Correlation and the capture ratios use.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\rho_k = \rho\big(r_i, r_{\text{fund}} \mid \text{block } k\big), \quad k = 1 \ldots n`} />

The window's paired returns are split into consecutive, non-overlapping
blocks of **30 returns each** — the same bar `MIN_OVERLAPPING_RETURNS`
already draws elsewhere in this app for "enough returns to trust a
correlation at all" (about six trading weeks), reused here as how long one
rolling reading should span rather than a second, unexplained number.
Blocks are counted back from the window's most recent return, so the last
block always ends on the window's own last date; only a leftover, oldest
partial block, if any, is dropped. Pearson correlation is computed
separately within each block, on raw (unscaled) paired returns — the same
kind Tail Correlation uses, since this is about which periods moved
together, not about comparing dispersion across gaps of different lengths.

**The column sorts and filters by the *change*, not by the most recent
reading**: the last block's ρ less the first block's. A holding at 0.85 that
was 0.5 three blocks ago sorts above a holding steady at 0.95 the whole
window, because +0.35 is a bigger *move toward* the fund than 0 is — this
column answers "is this relationship strengthening or weakening", not "what
does it stand at right now".

## How it reads

The sparkline is the path; the number beside it is the last block's ρ less
the first's. A rising line and a positive change mean the holding has been
moving with the fund more than it used to over the window; a falling line
and a negative change mean the reverse. A flat line near zero change means
the relationship has been stable, whatever level it sits at.

<Warning>
A rising line is a description of the window shown, not a forecast. Nothing
here predicts whether the trend continues, reverses, or was simply a
coincidence of the periods this window happened to cover — change the
window to see whether the same direction holds over a longer or shorter
stretch.
</Warning>

## Caveats

Null with a reason, not zero, whenever the window cannot support two full,
comparable blocks of 30 returns each, or whenever the earliest or the
latest block on its own had no variance to correlate — a short window (or a
holding that only recently listed) can leave too little history for a
"before" and "after" reading to exist at all, in which case there is no
path to draw and no change to report, rather than a flat line implying a
stability the window never actually demonstrated.
