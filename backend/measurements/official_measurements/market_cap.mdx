title: Market Cap
summary: Each holding's own market capitalisation, readable across a whole fund at once.

## What it measures

What the market as a whole thinks a holding is worth right now — its share
price times its shares outstanding. It says nothing about a holding's place
inside the fund (that is what % of ETF answers); it says how big the
company itself is, the same figure the holding's own popup already shows,
now sortable and filterable across every constituent at once.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for the fund's constituents, `stock_info` for each holding's own
metadata — name, sector, market cap, currency, exchange. `stock_info` reads
straight from the `ticker` table where a holding has been synced, falling
back to a live lookup otherwise; it takes no window, because a market cap
is a snapshot, not something measured over a stretch of history.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{market cap} = \text{stock\_info}(\text{ticker}).\texttt{marketCap}`} />

No arithmetic beyond a unit conversion: `stock_info` reports market cap in
raw dollars, and this column divides by a billion so the same $/B/T
thresholds the Value Held column already uses can be reused directly,
rather than every reader converting the exponent in their head.

## How it reads

A larger figure is a larger company, nothing more — it is not scaled by
how much of the fund that company represents, so a mega-cap making up 1%
of one fund and 8% of another shows the identical market cap in both. The
Cap-Weight Tilt column is the one that relates the two.

## Caveats

<Warning>
Null with a reason, not zero, for a holding `stock_info` has no market cap
for at all — a metadata field yfinance itself sometimes leaves blank, most
often for a holding that is itself a fund rather than a company. A missing
market cap here is exactly the case the Cap-Weight Tilt column excludes
from its own denominator.
</Warning>
