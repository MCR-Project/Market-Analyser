title: Dividend Yield & Income Share
summary: Trailing twelve-month income against a holding's price, and how much of its return came from being paid rather than from the price moving.
---

## What it measures

**Yield** is the plainest reading of income: every dividend declared over
the trailing twelve months, divided by the holding's own last close. It says
nothing about the price return over the same stretch — a holding can yield
well and still have lost money, or yield nothing and still have been the
best performer in the fund.

**Income Share** puts the two together: of the total return a holding
produced over the same twelve months — price change plus the income paid
along the way — what fraction was the income rather than the price move.
A holding that barely moved in price but paid steadily shows a high income
share; one that surged in price while paying little to nothing shows a low
one, even with an identical total return.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for the fund's constituents, `price_frame` for each holding's own
closes and `dividend_events` for its declared dividends, both restricted to
the same trailing twelve months. This is a fixed stretch, not the table's
shared window control — the two columns are defined against "trailing
twelve months" specifically, the same way `stock_info`'s market cap is
defined as "right now" regardless of what window the table happens to be
showing elsewhere.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{yield} = \frac{\sum \text{dividends}}{\text{last close}} \times 100`} />

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{income share} = \frac{\sum \text{dividends}}{(\text{last close} - \text{first close}) + \sum \text{dividends}} \times 100`} />

Dividend amounts are read exactly as the `dividends` table stores them —
declared, unadjusted — while the closes behind the price change are
adjusted, the same as every other price-derived figure in this app. That
mismatch is deliberate, the same one `services/portfolio.py`'s own income
figure carries: an adjusted close has already folded dividends back into
the series as reinvestment, so this column is a readable approximation of
where a holding's return came from, not a strict decomposition that sums
to the return exactly.

## How it reads

Yield alone is a snapshot of income relative to price today. Income Share
close to 100% means nearly all of the return came from being paid rather
than the price moving; close to 0% means the opposite; a number well above
100% or below 0% is not a bug — it means price and income pulled in
opposite directions over the window, so one had to overshoot the total to
cancel the other out.

## Caveats

<Warning>
Both are null with a reason, not zero, for a holding the `dividends`
table has no record of at all — every ETF, and anything resolved outside
the tracked universe. Reporting 0.00% would say the holding pays no
dividend, when the honest answer is that this app does not track one for
it.
</Warning>

Income Share is also null when price change and income net to exactly
zero over the window — there is no total return for income to be a share
of, so the ratio is undefined rather than a divide against nothing.
