title: Value Held
summary: Roughly how many dollars the fund has invested in one holding.

## What it measures

The same fact as **% of ETF**, in money rather than percent: about how much
of the fund's assets sit in this one company.

Percentages compare holdings within a fund; dollars compare across funds. A
2% position in a $800B fund is a far larger stake than a 20% position in a
$2B one, and only this column makes that obvious.

## Where the numbers come from

Two inputs:

- `etf_info` supplies the fund's assets under management, in billions of
  dollars, as the provider reports it.
- `holdings` supplies each constituent's weight.

Both come from the database, refreshed by the daily sync, with a live
lookup as a fallback.

## How it is computed

The fund's total assets, multiplied by the holding's share of them:

<Formula tex={String.raw`V_i = \mathrm{AUM} \times \frac{w_i}{100}`} />

with AUM in billions, so the result is in billions too. The cell then
formats it as `$16.3B`, `$980M` or `$1.24T` depending on size.

## How it reads

As the size of the fund's bet. It answers "if this company halved, how much
of the fund's money is exposed?" more directly than a percentage does.

It is also the honest way to compare a holding's treatment by two different
funds: the same stock might be 8% of a small sector ETF and 1% of a giant
index fund, while the index fund holds several times more of it in dollars.

## Caveats

<Warning>
This is an estimate, not a position report. It is derived from two published
figures — total assets and percentage weight — and is only ever as accurate
as the more stale of the two. Funds report AUM on their own schedule, and a
figure that lags a large inflow or a sharp drawdown scales *every* row in
this column by the same error.
</Warning>

- It assumes the reported weight still holds at the reported AUM. Between
  rebalances both drift with the market, and they are not necessarily
  measured on the same day.
- If AUM is missing for a fund, every value in the column is affected at
  once — the failure is not per-row, which makes it easy to miss.
- As with **% of ETF**, only tracked holdings appear, so the column does not
  account for the whole fund.
