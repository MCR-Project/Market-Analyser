title: "% of ETF"
summary: How much of the fund's portfolio a single holding accounts for.

## What it measures

The share of the fund that one holding represents, as the fund itself
reports it. A 7% weight means roughly seven cents of every dollar in the
fund is invested in that company.

This is the simplest column on the table — it is the fund's own published
weight, passed through — but it is the one that gives every other column its
context. A holding with a striking correlation matters much more at 7% than
at 0.4%.

## Where the numbers come from

One input: `holdings`, which returns the fund's constituents as
`[ticker, weight%]` pairs already sorted heaviest first.

Those weights originate from the fund provider's own published holdings
files, scraped by the fetchers in `fetcher/` and loaded into the database by
`scripts/complete_database.py`. The daily job refreshes them, so they track
the fund as it rebalances.

## How it reads

Read it as concentration. In a fund whose top holding is 7% and whose
twentieth is 1%, no single position dominates. In one whose top holding is
25%, the fund's behaviour is substantially that one company's behaviour,
whatever the other columns say.

Weights are the natural companion to **Value Held**, which turns the same
number into dollars using the fund's total assets.

## Caveats

<Warning>
**The column does not sum to 100%, and cannot.** Only stocks weighing at
least 1% in one of their tracked ETFs are kept in the database — lighter
ones are pruned. A broad index fund therefore shows a few dozen of its
several hundred constituents, and the weights shown add up to well under
100% of the fund.
</Warning>

- The 1% test is applied across *all* tracked funds, not per fund. A stock
  held at 0.3% here but 5% in another tracked ETF is kept, and appears here
  at its real 0.3%. That is why some rows sit below the threshold.
- Weights move with prices between rebalances, so they are as fresh as the
  last sync rather than exact at this instant.
- If the database has no holdings for a fund, the backend falls back to a
  live lookup that only exposes the top ten. The dashboard flags that
  state — the weights are real, but the list is truncated far harder than
  usual.
