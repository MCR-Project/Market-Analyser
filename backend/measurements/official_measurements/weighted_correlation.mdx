## What it measures

Each holding gets a single number: its average correlation to the other
holdings of its fund over the past year — but with each peer counting in
proportion to **its own weight in the fund**. Correlation to a position that
makes up a fifth of the fund moves the figure far more than correlation to one
that makes up a fraction of a percent.

The plain CORRELATION column gives every peer one vote. In a fund with one
giant and fifty small positions, that makes a holding's score mostly a
statement about the small ones. This column asks the other question: how
closely does the holding move with the positions that actually drive the fund?

A high value means it has been moving with the heavy positions. A low value
means it has been drifting away from them, whatever it did against the long
tail.

## Where the numbers come from

Two inputs, both fetched by the measurement itself — the same two the plain
column reads, so enabling this one costs no extra price history:

- `holdings` gives the fund's constituents and their weights. Only the tickers
  tracked in the database appear, unless the fund has been deep-filled — see the
  caveats.
- `correlation_matrix` turns those tickers into a matrix of pairwise
  correlations over **one year** of **daily** returns. Nothing in the UI
  mentions that window and this column cannot change it.

## How it is computed

For holding <Formula inline tex={String.raw`i`} />, the weighted mean of its
correlations to every peer <Formula inline tex={String.raw`j`} />, using the
**peer's** fund weight <Formula inline tex={String.raw`w_j`} />:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\tilde\rho_i = \frac{\sum_{j \neq i} w_j \, \rho_{ij}}{\sum_{j \neq i} w_j}`} />

- The holding itself is never counted: the sums skip
  <Formula inline tex={String.raw`j = i`} />. So a holding's own weight changes
  its peers' scores and never its own.
- A pair with no computed correlation is left out **together with its weight**.
  It does not count as zero, which would pull the score down by exactly the
  weight of the peer the data says least about.
- A holding with no computed pair at all has no figure — a dash, with a reason.

A small case, worth checking by hand. Holding C has ρ = 0.8 to A and ρ = 0.2
to B, and A weighs 50 and B weighs 10 in the fund:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\tilde\rho_C = \frac{50 \cdot 0.8 + 10 \cdot 0.2}{50 + 10} = \frac{42}{60} = 0.70`} />

The plain average of the same two pairs is 0.50. The difference is the whole
point: C moves closely with the position that matters and loosely with the one
that barely does, and only the weighted figure says so.

## How it reads

| Weighted ρ | What it usually indicates |
| --- | --- |
| Above 0.5 | Moves closely with the fund's heaviest positions |
| 0.2 – 0.5 | Typical for a sector fund, where one theme drives everything |
| 0.0 – 0.2 | Largely independent of the positions that drive the fund |
| Below 0 | Has tended to move against them |

Read it beside CORRELATION, not instead of it. A weighted figure well above the
plain one says the holding tracks the big names more tightly than it tracks the
tail; a weighted figure well below says it has been moving with the tail and
away from the big names.

<Note>
It weights the *peers*; it does not say how much the holding itself matters to
the fund. That is its weight, shown in the % OF ETF column.
</Note>

## Caveats

<Warning>
This describes the past year. It is not a prediction, and correlations
notoriously rise in a sell-off — precisely when the diversification a low
number seems to promise would matter most.
</Warning>

- **The fund is its tracked holdings only**, not its full constituent list —
  unless the fund has been deep-filled, in which case the figure covers the
  whole fund for as long as that lasts. Outside a deep-fill, the peers are the
  larger positions the app tracks, and the tail that carries the rest of the
  weight is not in the sum.
- **There is no floor on how little peer weight a score may rest on.** A holding
  whose only computed pair is to a position weighing 0.2% scores that one
  correlation, in full. A pair with fewer than 30 overlapping daily returns is
  unknown, not zero, so a holding that is new can end up with a score resting on
  very little.
- **A holding with no computed pair to any weighted peer shows a dash** rather
  than a value, with the reason beside it.
- **Holdings with no price history at all are dropped** before the matrix is
  built, so they get no value rather than a wrong one.
