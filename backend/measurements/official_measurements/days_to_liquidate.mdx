---
title: Days to Liquidate
summary: How many normal trading days it would take to sell the fund's entire position in a holding.
---

## What it measures

Value Held says a fund holds, say, $61.8B of a stock. It says nothing about
whether that position could actually be sold. For a large position in a
thinly traded name, that is the more interesting fact. This column divides
the value held by the holding's own average daily dollar volume, giving the
position's size in the plainest possible unit: days of normal trading.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for each ticker's weight, `etf_info` for the fund's AUM,
`price_frame` for closes and volume together — the bulk, volume-widened
read (issue #102), since this is the first official column to actually read
`prices.volume` rather than a call per holding.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{days} = \frac{\text{value held}}{\text{average daily dollar volume}}`} />

Value held is exactly the same arithmetic the Value Held column itself
uses — fund AUM times weight — so the two columns cannot disagree. Average
daily dollar volume is the mean, across the window, of each priced date's
`close × volume`, with one correction described below.

## The bucket correction

`prices.volume` is a whole bucket's **sum**, not one day's — about 21
trading days for a monthly bucket, the reason that column stayed a
`bigint` in the first place. Averaging bucketed rows straight would read a
monthly sum as though it were a single day's total, overstating every
position's liquidity by roughly the width of its bucket — a holding priced
in monthly rows would look about twenty times more liquid than it is.

Each row is instead divided by the trading days the gap back to the row
before it actually covers, before the average is taken — the same
conversion every other bucket-spanning figure in this app already uses
(`services.stats.trading_days`). A window mixing daily, weekly and monthly
rows (issue #10) corrects each one by its own bucket's width rather than
applying one blanket adjustment to the whole series.

## How it reads

A holding under a day or two is liquid relative to the position — the fund
could unwind it in the ordinary course of a session or two. A holding in
the double digits is a position that would take real time to exit at
anything resembling its own average trading pace.

<Warning>
This is a scale, not a plan. It assumes today's average volume continues
unchanged and takes no account of market impact — actually selling a
position this size would itself move the price and very likely draw the
average down further, the opposite of the assumption the number rests on.
It is a way to compare positions against each other, not a timeline for
unwinding one.
</Warning>

## Caveats

Null with a reason, not zero, for a holding the `prices` table has no rows
for at all — every ETF, and anything resolved outside the tracked
universe, the same holdings `price_frame`'s own volume field is null for.
A holding priced for fewer than two dates has no gap to measure even one
bucket's width from, and is null for the same reason.

This column is out of scope for anything beyond the ratio itself: no
liquidity score or composite rating, and no bid-ask spread or intraday
data, none of which this app's pipeline carries.
