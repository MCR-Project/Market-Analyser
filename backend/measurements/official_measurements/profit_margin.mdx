title: Profit Margin
summary: How many cents of each dollar of revenue this holding keeps as profit.

## What it measures

Net profit margin — net income divided by revenue, trailing twelve months.
A company keeping 20 cents of profit on every dollar of sales has a 20%
margin; a thin-margin business might keep 2 cents. Margin alone says
nothing about size or growth, only how much of each sale turns into
profit.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` resolves the fund's holding list; `fundamentals` reads each
holding's `profitMargins` straight from yfinance's own `.info`
(`measurements/inputs/fundamentals.py`), live, with no database path.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Revenue}}`} />

Read as-is from Yahoo and expressed as a percentage, rounded to two decimal
places.

## How it reads

A healthy margin varies enormously by industry — a grocery chain and a
software company are not on the same scale — so this is far more useful
compared against a peer or against the same holding's own history than
read as an absolute number.

## Caveats

<Warning>
A negative margin means the company lost money over the trailing twelve
months, not that the figure is missing — it is shown in red, not as a
dash.
</Warning>

Null, not zero, for a holding Yahoo reports nothing for (an ETF held inside
another ETF) — a zero would claim a company broke exactly even.
