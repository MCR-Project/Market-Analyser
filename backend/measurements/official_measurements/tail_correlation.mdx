---
title: Tail Correlation
summary: Correlation to the fund on its own worst periods — whether a holding still moves with it on the days that hurt.
---

## What it measures

An ordinary correlation is an average over the whole window — it can hide a
relationship that holds up fine on a calm day and comes apart exactly when
it would matter most. This computes correlation only over the fund's own
worst decile of periods by return: does this holding still move with the
fund when the fund is falling hardest, or does the relationship break down
right when a reader would most want to rely on it?

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for each ticker's weight, `price_frame` for closes, `fund_index`
for the fund's own weighted-return series — the same benchmark Beta/R²/
Idiosyncratic Volatility and the capture ratios use.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{tail}_i = \rho\big(r_i, r_{\text{fund}} \mid r_{\text{fund}} \in \text{worst decile}\big)`} />

The fund's own periods are ranked by return and the worst 10% are kept;
Pearson correlation is then computed on exactly that slice, using raw
(unscaled) paired returns — the same kind the capture ratios use, since this
is about which periods were worst by how much they actually moved, not
about comparing dispersion across gaps of different lengths.

## How it reads

Close to the holding's own full-window correlation: the relationship is
stable, calm or stressed. Meaningfully lower (or negative) in the tail than
elsewhere: the holding decouples from the fund exactly when the fund is
struggling — a diversification story that only shows up under stress, which
a full-window correlation would never reveal on its own.

## Caveats

<Warning>
"Worst decile" is a decile **of periods actually observed**, not of
calendar time. `prices` tiers by age (issue #10) — a 1-5 year window answers
in weekly buckets — so for most windows on this table, "the fund's worst
decile" means its worst *weeks*, not its worst days. Read the column
header's window before treating this as a daily-stress figure.
</Warning>

Null with a reason, not zero, when fewer than two periods fall in the tail
after the cutoff, or when the tail itself turns out to have no variance to
correlate — a short window can leave too few periods for a decile to mean
anything.
