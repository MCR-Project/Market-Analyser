## What it measures

Each holding gets a single number: the average correlation between its daily
returns and those of every other holding in the same fund, over the past
year.

A high value means the stock has been moving with the pack — when the fund
goes up, it goes up. A low value means it has been drifting on its own, and
a negative one means it has tended to move against its peers. It is a
description of how much *independent* movement a holding has contributed to
the fund.

## Where the numbers come from

Two inputs, both fetched by the measurement itself:

- `holdings` gives the fund's constituents. Only the tickers tracked in the
  database appear, so a fund's full constituent list is not what gets
  correlated — see the caveats.
- `correlation_matrix` turns those tickers into a matrix of pairwise
  correlations. Its defaults are the important part, because nothing in the
  UI mentions them: **one year** of history at **daily** granularity.

Daily closing prices are converted to daily percentage returns, and the
correlation is computed on the returns — not on the prices themselves.
Correlating price levels would mostly measure that two stocks both drifted
upward all year.

## How it is computed

Pairwise Pearson correlation across every pair of holdings, then, for each
holding, the mean of its correlations to all the others:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\bar\rho_i = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j \neq i} \rho_{ij}`} />

A holding's correlation with itself is always 1, and it is excluded — the
sum skips <Formula inline tex={String.raw`j = i`} /> and the average divides
by <Formula inline tex={String.raw`n - 1`} />, not <Formula inline tex={String.raw`n`} />.
Including it would inflate every value by the same amount and flatter small
funds most.

## How it reads

| Average ρ | What it usually indicates |
| --- | --- |
| Above 0.5 | Moves closely with the fund; adds little diversification |
| 0.2 – 0.5 | Typical for a sector fund, where one theme drives everything |
| 0.0 – 0.2 | Largely independent of its peers |
| Below 0 | Has tended to move against the rest of the fund |

What counts as "high" depends entirely on the fund. In a semiconductor ETF
almost everything is driven by the same demand cycle, so 0.4 is ordinary; in
a broad market index the same 0.4 would stand out.

<Note>
The value is an average, so it hides its own spread. A holding correlated
0.9 to two peers and −0.1 to eight others reports the same 0.1 as one
sitting flat against all of them. The matrix view shows the pairs behind the
average.
</Note>

## Caveats

<Warning>
This describes the past year. It is not a prediction, and correlations
notoriously rise in a sell-off — precisely when the diversification a low
number seems to promise would matter most.
</Warning>

- **A pair with fewer than 30 overlapping daily returns — about six trading
  weeks — is unknown, not zero.** A holding correlated with nothing shows a
  dash rather than a value, and an unknown pair is excluded from every
  average, from the strongest/weakest pair, and from the hub — it is never
  ranked as though it were the most independent holding in the fund just
  because it is the newest.
- **Holdings with no price history at all are dropped** before the matrix is
  built, so they get no value rather than a wrong one.
- The fund is only its *tracked* holdings, not its full constituent list, so
  in a broad index this is the average correlation to the largest holdings
  rather than to all several hundred.
