title: Total Debt

## What it measures

A company's total debt — its short-term and long-term borrowings added
together, as of its most recent reported quarter. Says nothing by itself
about whether that debt is a problem: a capital-intensive business
carrying debt against real assets is a different story from a company
borrowing to cover losses, and this column does not tell them apart.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` resolves the fund's holding list; `fundamentals` reads each
holding's `totalDebt` straight from yfinance's own `.info`
(`measurements/inputs/fundamentals.py`), live, with no database path.

## How it is computed

Read as-is from Yahoo, in US dollars, then divided into billions and
rounded to two decimal places — the same scaling Market Cap uses, so a
debt figure and a market cap read the same way side by side.

## How it reads

Debt means little without something to compare it against — a holding's
own market cap or revenue, or how that debt has changed over recent
quarters. A large absolute number can be unremarkable for a very large
company and alarming for a small one.

## Caveats

<Warning>
A genuinely debt-free company reports $0, not a dash — the two mean
different things here, and only Yahoo having no figure at all (an ETF
held inside another ETF) reads as a dash.
</Warning>
