summary: How much of the fund's own return a holding captured on the way up, and on the way down — two different questions one average return can't separate.

## What it measures

A holding that returns the same as its fund over a window can have gotten
there two very different ways: rising and falling right alongside it, or
sitting flat and then catching one sharp rally. Upside capture asks how much
of the fund's own compounded return a holding captured over exactly the
periods the fund rose; downside capture asks the same over exactly the
periods it fell. 100% on both means the holding simply mirrors the fund;
below 100% on the downside and near or above 100% on the upside is the
combination most readers are actually hoping to see.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for each ticker's weight, `price_frame` for closes, `fund_index`
for the fund's own weighted-return series — the same benchmark Beta/R²/
Idiosyncratic Volatility and Tail Correlation use.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{up} = \frac{\prod_{r_{\text{fund}} > 0} (1 + r_i) - 1}{\prod_{r_{\text{fund}} > 0} (1 + r_{\text{fund}}) - 1} \times 100`} />

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{down} = \frac{\prod_{r_{\text{fund}} < 0} (1 + r_i) - 1}{\prod_{r_{\text{fund}} < 0} (1 + r_{\text{fund}}) - 1} \times 100`} />

Each ratio compounds the holding's own returns over exactly the periods
selected by the fund's own sign — never blended, never over the whole
window — as a percentage of the fund's own compounded return over that same
selection.

## How it reads

| Up capture | Down capture | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ≥ 100% | < 100% | Participates in rallies, cushions declines — the ideal combination |
| ≥ 100% | ≥ 100% | Amplifies the fund in both directions |
| < 100% | < 100% | Dampens the fund in both directions — a defensive holding |

## Caveats

Each ratio is null, not zero, when the fund never had an up (or down)
period at all in the window — there is nothing to capture a share of.
Computed from raw, unscaled paired returns rather than the trading-time-
scaled ones Beta/Idiosyncratic Volatility use: a capture ratio compares
like return to like return over one identical stretch, not a dispersion
figure needing a common time footing first.

<Warning>
A short window, or one dominated by a single long rally or decline, can
leave very few periods behind either ratio — a capture ratio built from a
handful of periods is a much noisier estimate than the same figure over a
full cycle.
</Warning>
