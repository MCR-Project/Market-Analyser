---
title: Cap-Weight Tilt
summary: How far a holding's fund weight sits from what a plain cap-weighted basket would have given it.
---

## What it measures

A cap-weighted basket gives every constituent exactly its share of the
group's total market value — the passive, no-opinion allocation. This
column is how far the fund's own weight strays from that: positive means
the fund holds more of a name than a cap-weighted ranking would, negative
means less. A fund tracking a cap-weighted index sits near zero on every
row; one screening, tilting or equal-weighting shows real numbers.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for the fund's own weights, `stock_info` for each holding's
market cap. No window: both are snapshots, not history.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{tilt}_i = w_i - \frac{\text{cap}_i}{\sum_j \text{cap}_j}\times 100`} />

The denominator sums market cap only over the fund's own **tracked**
holdings — the constituents weighing at least 1% that this app's scrapers
actually record, not the fund's full, often much longer, constituent
list. That means the "cap-weighted basket" a tilt is measured against is
itself restricted to names already on this table, not the real index's
full universe: a fund whose long tail is entirely sub-1% positions
compares its top holdings against a slightly smaller and slightly
differently-shaped basket than the fund's own prospectus would.

## How it reads

| Tilt | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Near 0pp | This holding's weight is close to what a plain cap-weighted ranking would give it |
| Well above 0pp | The fund deliberately overweights this name relative to its market size |
| Well below 0pp | The fund deliberately underweights it, or excludes it from a cap-weighted peer's usual share |

Summed across a fund's tracked holdings, tilts land close to zero — not
exactly, since the tracked set's own weights do not sum to exactly 100%
either (measurements/inputs/holdings.py's own 1%-cutoff rule).

## Caveats

<Warning>
The tracked-set denominator means this column cannot see a fund's
untracked tail at all. A fund built from hundreds of sub-1% positions
looks, by this column alone, like a basket of only its largest names —
correct about the holdings it can see, silent about everything below the
cutoff.
</Warning>

Null with a reason, not zero, for a holding with no market cap on record —
answering it with 0pp would claim the fund's weight exactly matches a
cap-weighted basket, rather than that the comparison could not be made.
