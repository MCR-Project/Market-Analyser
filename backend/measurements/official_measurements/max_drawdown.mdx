---
title: Max Drawdown
summary: The deepest fall from a prior peak in a holding's own price history over the window.
---

## What it measures

The single worst peak-to-trough fall the holding's own price took inside
the window — never a positive number, and 0% is a real answer (the holding
never fell below a prior peak in the window), not a missing one. Two
holdings with the same total return can have taken very different paths to
get there; this is the one that says how rough the ride was, not just where
it ended up.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` for the fund's constituents, `price_frame` for each holding's own
closes over the table's shared window. No fund-level data.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{drawdown} = \min_t \left( \frac{P_t - \max_{s \le t} P_s}{\max_{s \le t} P_s} \right)`} />

`services.stats.max_drawdown` walks the series once, tracking the running
peak so far and the worst fall from it seen at any point — the same
function the portfolio simulator's own Max Drawdown metric applies to a
portfolio's own series.

## How it reads

Closer to 0% means a smoother ride; further below means a rougher one. It
says nothing about how long the fall took, or how long it took to recover —
only how deep it went.

## Caveats

Null with a reason, not zero, for a holding with fewer than two priced
dates over the window — there has to be at least a peak and a later point
to measure a fall between.

<Warning>
This is the deepest fall **inside the window shown**. A holding that fell
further just before the window started, or that is still underwater from a
fall that began earlier, will not show that history here — change the
window to look further back.
</Warning>
