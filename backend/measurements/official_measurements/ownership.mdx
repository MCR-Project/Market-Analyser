## What it measures

Two shares of a company's stock: the fraction insiders (officers,
directors, large individual holders required to report) hold, and the
fraction institutions (funds, pensions, banks) hold. Neither is a
judgement — high insider ownership can mean confidence or it can mean a
founder-controlled company; high institutional ownership can mean broad
index inclusion as easily as active conviction.

## Where the numbers come from

`holdings` resolves the fund's holding list; `fundamentals` reads each
holding's `heldPercentInsiders` and `heldPercentInstitutions` straight from
yfinance's own `.info` (`measurements/inputs/fundamentals.py`), live, with
no database path — these are Yahoo's own periodically-refreshed estimate,
not a figure this app tracks a history of.

## How it is computed

Both fractions are read as-is and expressed as a percentage, rounded to two
decimal places.

## How it reads

Neither column alone says whether a company is well- or poorly-owned.
Reading them together with a company's size is more useful: very high
insider ownership in a small company often means a founder or family still
controls it; very high institutional ownership in a large one usually just
means it is in the index funds everyone holds.

## Caveats

<Warning>
An ETF held as a constituent of another ETF has no insiders or
institutional holders of its own kind to report, and reads as a dash here
— not zero, which would claim no one holds it.
</Warning>
