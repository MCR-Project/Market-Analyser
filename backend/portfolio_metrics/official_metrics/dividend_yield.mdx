## What it measures

The same fact Dividend Income reports, scaled to be comparable across
portfolios of very different sizes — $500 of income means something very
different to a $10,000 account than to a $1,000,000 one, and this is the
figure that says which.

Like Dividend Income, this never shows as a toggleable tile: it appears in
`PortfolioSummary`'s prose note instead (issue #68), as the percentage
figure in the sentence stating how much of the total is income.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{dividendYield} = \frac{\text{dividendIncome}}{\text{totalInvested}} \times 100`} />

Divided by everything paid in, not just the opening amount — a portfolio
funded once a month for five years and one funded with a single lump sum
of the same eventual size should not be credited with the same yield if
only one of them actually held that much money the whole time.

## How it reads

Treat it the way a dividend yield is normally read — a rough sense of how
income-oriented the portfolio is — but remember this is a total-return
app: the yield does not need to be reinvested by hand the way it would for
a real brokerage account, because `prices`' adjusted closes already did
that automatically.

## Caveats

Only speaks for holdings the `dividends` table has a record of. See
Dividend Income and Income Unknown For for what "not on record" means and
which holdings it applies to.
