---
title: Money-Weighted Return
summary: The annual rate that reconciles every deposit, from the day it arrived, with the final value.
---

## What it measures

What the *money* itself earned, given exactly when each dollar showed up
— the internal rate of return (IRR). $1,000 that became $1,100 after a
$100 deposit landed last week has not returned 10%; this is the figure
that answers what it actually returned once the timing of that deposit is
taken into account, which Total Return (deliberately) does not.

## How it is computed

Every cash movement is signed from the holder's point of view — negative
going in, one positive flow at the end for what it all became — and this
is the single annual rate that discounts every one of them, from the day
it happened, to zero:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\sum_{i} \frac{\text{flow}_i}{(1+r)^{\text{years}_i}} = 0`} />

Solved by bisection rather than a closed form, over a 365.25-day year —
the same year length `CAGR` compounds over, so the two rates answer
different questions on the same calendar.

## How it reads

This is the account's own return, comparable to what an alternative use
of that exact same money, on the exact same dates, would have needed to
earn to match it. With no contributions it equals CAGR exactly, by
construction — the two only diverge once money keeps arriving.

## Caveats

Needs a genuine sign change and elapsed time to solve for: a window too
short, or a loss so total no annual rate discounts the deposits down to
what came back, reports null rather than guessing. A portfolio that ended
at essentially nothing reports −100% — every dollar is gone, and no rate
describes that more honestly than all of it.
