## What it measures

The money that left the account through a recurring withdrawal: the
account-side counterpart to Contributed, for a portfolio that is drawn on
instead of paid into. A portfolio does one or the other, never both, so at
most one of the two is ever above zero.

## How it is computed

One withdrawal comes out on the first trading row of every new period
(monthly, quarterly or yearly) after the window's own first row — never on
the first row itself, which is the opening amount. It comes out of every
holding, and of any cash still waiting for a holding to list, in proportion
to what each is worth at that moment, so a withdrawal changes how much the
portfolio holds and leaves its mix alone:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{withdrawn} = \sum \text{amount actually taken}`} />

*Actually taken* matters. A portfolio that cannot cover a withdrawal gives up
whatever is left and pays nothing after that, so a schedule of $600 a month
against a $1,000 balance takes $600 and then $400 — $1,000 in all, not
$1,800.

## How it reads

Zero with no schedule set, which is the default. Paid In does not shrink
by this amount: it stays the opening amount, because netting the two would
let it go negative for a portfolio drawn on for longer than it was funded.
Gain adds this figure back — money that was taken out was still earned, so
a portfolio that returned $2,000 and paid $1,500 of it to its holder made
$2,000, not $500.

## Caveats

When the portfolio runs out, nothing else here says so: Total Return and
the other portfolio-family figures are read off a value that a withdrawal
does not move, so a portfolio drawn to nothing on flat prices reports a
return of zero. The date it ran out is reported alongside, and null there
means the money lasted — it does not mean the date is unknown.

Says nothing about *when* each withdrawal came out relative to the
market, only the total. Money-Weighted Return is the figure that accounts
for timing; this one is a plain sum.
