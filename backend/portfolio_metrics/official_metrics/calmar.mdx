---
title: Calmar Ratio
summary: CAGR divided by the depth of the worst drawdown.
---

## What it measures

A portfolio that grew faster is not automatically the better one to have
held if it fell much further to get there. Calmar is the plainest way to
weigh the two against each other: how much the portfolio compounded,
relative to how deep its own worst fall went.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{Calmar} = \frac{\text{CAGR}}{|\text{max drawdown}|}`} />

Both figures already sit in this same `metrics` object — Calmar is not a
second pass over the price series, only a ratio of two numbers already
computed for the CAGR and Max Drawdown tiles.

## How it reads

Higher is better: more compounding for every point of depth the worst
fall reached. A Calmar under 1 means the worst drawdown, in percentage
terms, was actually larger than the annual growth rate that got the
portfolio through it — a rough ride relative to the reward.

## Caveats

<Warning>
Null when the run never fell below a prior peak at all — not because the
run did badly, but because a ratio against a drawdown of exactly zero is
not a number, however good the CAGR behind it. This is the one case where
a strong run reports a dash rather than an exceptionally high figure.
</Warning>

Also null wherever CAGR itself is: a window with no elapsed time to
compound a growth rate over.
