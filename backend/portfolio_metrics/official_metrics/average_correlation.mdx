---
title: Average Correlation
summary: The average pairwise correlation among this basket's own holdings.
---

## What it measures

A basket of six holdings that all rise and fall together is one bet
wearing six names, however different their weights look on the table.
Average correlation is the plainest way to see that: across every pair
of holdings in the basket, how closely did they actually move together?

Lower means more diversified — the holdings are doing genuinely different
things. A reading near 1.0 means the basket's real number of independent
positions is much smaller than its holding count suggests.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\bar\rho = \frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}} \sum_{i<j} \operatorname{Corr}(r_i, r_j)`} />

The mean of every distinct pair's own Pearson correlation, each computed
from the same trading-time-scaled returns `volatility` and the risk share
below are built from, over the same joint covariance matrix
`riskShare`/`effectiveBets` read — so all three describe the same window
rather than three that happen to agree. A holding whose own variance is
zero drops only its own pairs from the average rather than nulling the
whole figure.

## How it reads

Close to 0 or negative is the diversified end — the holdings are
genuinely offsetting each other's moves. Close to 1 means the basket's
apparent breadth is mostly nominal: many names, one real exposure.

## Caveats

<Note>
Negative here is not "bad" the way a negative return is — it is the more
diversified end of the same number a positive reading is the less
diversified end of. This tile is never tone-coloured for that reason.
</Note>

Null for a basket of one holding — there is no pair to correlate at all
— and under the same incomplete-history or no-variance conditions
`riskShare`/`effectiveBets` are. Averaging away every pair also hides
which *specific* pair is driving the number up; Risk Share is the figure
for which holdings actually carry the basket's risk.
