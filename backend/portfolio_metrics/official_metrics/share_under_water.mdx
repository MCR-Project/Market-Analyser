---
title: Share Under Water
summary: What fraction of the whole window was spent below a prior peak.
---

## What it measures

Time Under Water reports the longest single stretch the portfolio spent
below a prior high-water mark. This is that figure's own companion: not
the longest stretch, but the *total* time spent below a peak across the
whole window, as a share of it. A portfolio that fell once and spent most
of the window recovering reads very differently here than one that dipped
and recovered repeatedly, even if their longest single stretch was the
same length.

## How it is computed

Every distinct underwater stretch in the window (the same set Time Under
Water finds its longest member from) has its own duration summed, then
divided by the window's own elapsed days:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{share} = \frac{\sum \text{stretch durations}}{\text{window's own elapsed days}} \times 100`} />

Stretches never overlap, so summing every one double-counts nothing. A
stretch still open at the window's last row is timed only as far as it has
actually been observed, the same rule Time Under Water follows.

## How it reads

Close to 0% means the portfolio spent nearly the whole window at or near
its own high-water mark. Close to 100% means it spent almost the entire
window recovering from something, whatever that fall's own depth was —
this figure says nothing about how deep, only for how long.

## Caveats

0% is a real, common answer — it means the portfolio never fell below a
prior peak in this window at all. Null only when the window itself has
fewer than two rows of price history to measure an elapsed day from, the
same floor Time Under Water reports null for.
