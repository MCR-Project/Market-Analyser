title: Gain
summary: What the portfolio actually made — final value, plus anything taken out, less everything paid into it.
---

## What it measures

The plain dollar profit: what the account is worth now, plus whatever was
taken out of it along the way, minus every dollar that was ever put into
it. Where Total Return asks "how did the investing go" with deposits
stripped out, Gain asks the more literal question — is there more money
here than was paid in, and how much more.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{gain} = \text{finalValue} + \text{withdrawn} - \text{totalInvested}`} />

Withdrawn is zero for a portfolio with no recurring withdrawal, which is
the default, and then this is simply what it is worth less what went in.
Money taken out is added back because it was still earned: a portfolio that
returned $2,000 and paid $1,500 of it to its holder made $2,000, not $500.

## How it reads

A positive Gain with a negative Total Return (or the reverse) is possible
once contributions are on — Gain can be pulled up by fresh money arriving
even in a falling market, while Total Return, reading the flow-free unit
value, is not. Read the two together rather than expecting them to agree.

## Caveats

Once a rebalance starts moving money between holdings, each holding's own
`contribution` field is the only way to say which one earned which part
of this total — Gain itself is a portfolio-wide figure and does not
attribute credit to any one position. A holding's own figure follows the
same rule: what it is worth, plus what was taken out of it, less what went
into it.
