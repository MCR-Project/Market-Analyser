## What it measures

The plain dollar profit: what the account is worth now, minus every
dollar that was ever put into it. Where Total Return asks "how did the
investing go" with deposits stripped out, Gain asks the more literal
question — is there more money here than was paid in, and how much more.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{gain} = \text{finalValue} - \text{totalInvested}`} />

## How it reads

A positive Gain with a negative Total Return (or the reverse) is possible
once contributions are on — Gain can be pulled up by fresh money arriving
even in a falling market, while Total Return, reading the flow-free unit
value, is not. Read the two together rather than expecting them to agree.

## Caveats

Once a rebalance starts moving money between holdings, each holding's own
`contribution` field is the only way to say which one earned which part
of this total — Gain itself is a portfolio-wide figure and does not
attribute credit to any one position.
