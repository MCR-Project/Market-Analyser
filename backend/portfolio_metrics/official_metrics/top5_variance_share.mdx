---
title: Top 5 Variance Share
summary: How much of the fund's own risk its five biggest risk contributors account for — not necessarily its five biggest positions.
---

## What it measures

Not every holding contributes risk in proportion to its weight. A small,
volatile position that moves opposite the rest of the basket can add
almost nothing to the fund's own swings; a merely mid-sized one that
moves with everything else can dominate them. This figure sums the five
largest of those actual contributions — each holding's own share of the
fund's total variance — rather than the five largest positions by
weight.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{share} = \sum_{i \in \text{top 5}} \frac{w_i \cdot \text{marginal}_i}{\sigma^2_{\text{fund}}}`} />

Each holding's share comes from `services.stats.risk_contribution` — the
Euler decomposition of variance, weight times marginal contribution to
variance, over the fund's total variance — computed from the same joint
covariance matrix Diversification Ratio reads. The five largest of those
shares are summed here; every tracked holding's share still sums to 100%
across the whole fund, this is just the top slice of it.

## How it reads

A high share means the fund's risk is concentrated in a handful of
holdings, even if its weight looks spread across many more — the kind of
concentration a plain "top 5 by weight" figure can miss entirely when
the biggest positions happen to be the calmer ones. A low share means
the fund's risk is genuinely distributed across more than a handful of
names.

## Caveats

This measures risk concentration, not weight concentration — it can be
high even in a fund whose top five *positions* are unremarkable, if
those happen to be the five holdings actually driving its swings. Like
Diversification Ratio, it is computed only from the fund's tracked
holdings and the window they share; see Tracked Weight Coverage for how
much of the fund that excludes.
