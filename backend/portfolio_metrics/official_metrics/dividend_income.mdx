title: Dividend Income

## What it measures

How much of the portfolio's value came from being paid, rather than from
prices moving — read on its own, never added to Final Value, since
`prices` stores dividend-adjusted closes and the payout is already
counted inside every figure above the moment it arrived.

This is the one metric on this page that never shows as a toggleable
tile: `PortfolioSummary` prints it as a sentence under the tile grid
instead (see "Caveats"), because the fact worth stating is a
*relationship* — how much of what is already there came from income — not
a number that invites being added on top of Final Value.

## How it is computed

One event at a time, on the shares actually held going into each ex-date
— not the shares held at the *start* of the window, since buy and hold
drifts, a rebalance resets it and a contribution grows it:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{dividendIncome} = \sum_{\text{ticker}} \sum_{\text{events in window}} \text{shares\_held} \times \text{amount}`} />

## How it reads

A meaningful number for an income-oriented holding, closer to zero for a
pure growth one — neither is wrong, they are different kinds of
portfolio. Read Dividend Yield alongside it for the same fact as a
percentage of everything paid in, which is comparable across portfolios
of very different sizes the way a raw dollar figure is not.

## Caveats

<Note>
`PortfolioSummary.jsx` renders this as prose, under the tile grid, on
purpose (issue #68): "DIVIDEND INCOME $1,234" sitting beside "FINAL VALUE"
invites exactly the addition this note exists to prevent. Look for it in
the sentence beginning "These are total returns."
</Note>

Only speaks for holdings the `dividends` table has a record of — every
ETF, and anything resolved outside the tracked universe, is named in
Income Unknown For instead of silently read as having paid nothing.
