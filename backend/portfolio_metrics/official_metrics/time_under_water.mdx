title: Time Under Water
summary: The longest single stretch the run spent below a prior peak before recovering.

## What it measures

Max Drawdown says how deep the worst fall went. It says nothing about how
long the portfolio stayed down there — a 40% fall that recovered in two
months and one that took three years report the identical −40%, and the
difference between them is most of what it actually felt like to hold
through. This is the duration Max Drawdown leaves out: the longest single
stretch, in calendar days, spent below a prior high-water mark before
climbing back to it.

## How it is computed

Measured on the flow-free unit value, the same series every other
portfolio-family figure reads:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{stretch} = \{t : \text{unitValue}_t < \max_{s \le t}(\text{unitValue}_s)\}`} />

Every distinct underwater stretch in the window is found first — a run
below the peak, ended the first time the series climbs back to (or past)
it — and this figure reports the longest one's own duration: its end date
less its start date. A stretch still open at the window's last row (the
portfolio has not, as of the last thing observed, made its old peak back)
is timed only as far as it has actually been observed, not guessed at.

## How it reads

A short number next to a deep Max Drawdown is a sharp fall that recovered
quickly — uncomfortable, brief. A long number next to a shallow one is a
grind: nothing dramatic happened, but the portfolio simply took a long
time to get back to even. Share Under Water is this figure's own
companion, filed alongside it — the *total* time spent below a peak
across the whole window, not just the single longest stretch of it.

## Caveats

0 days is a real, common answer — it means the portfolio never fell below
a prior peak in this window at all, not that the figure could not be
computed. Null only when the window itself has fewer than two rows of
price history to measure an elapsed day from. Like Max Drawdown, this is
measured on the portfolio's own total, never on one holding in isolation.
