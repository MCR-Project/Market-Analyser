---
title: Pain Index
summary: The mean drawdown depth across the whole window, weighted by how long each one persisted.
---

## What it measures

Max Drawdown is "how much, once" — the single worst fall. This is "how
much, on average" — every day of the window's own distance below its
running peak, averaged across the whole stretch rather than reduced to
its single worst moment. A portfolio that fell 40% once and spent a month
there scores very differently here than one that sat 10% below its own
peak for most of a year, even if the second one's Max Drawdown is far
shallower.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{pain} = \frac{\sum_t \left|\frac{\text{unitValue}_t - \max_{s \le t}(\text{unitValue}_s)}{\max_{s \le t}(\text{unitValue}_s)}\right| \times \text{days}_t}{\sum_t \text{days}_t}`} />

Each row's own drawdown from the running peak is weighted by how many
calendar days it persisted before the next observation, not averaged one
row, one vote — a window spanning storage tiers has far fewer rows in its
oldest, coarsest stretch however long that stretch actually lasted, and an
unweighted average would under-count exactly the years answered in
monthly buckets.

## How it reads

Closer to 0% means the portfolio spent most of the window at or near its
own high-water mark. A pain index well below the Max Drawdown figure
means the worst fall was a brief exception rather than the window's own
character; one close to it means the portfolio spent much of its time
about as far down as it ever got.

## Caveats

<Note>
Zero here is a genuine, common answer — a portfolio that never fell below
a prior peak scores exactly 0%, the same as Max Drawdown does for the
same run, not a missing or unsupported figure.
</Note>

Null only for a window with no elapsed day between any two rows at all —
a single row of price history. Measured on the portfolio's own total,
never on one holding in isolation.
