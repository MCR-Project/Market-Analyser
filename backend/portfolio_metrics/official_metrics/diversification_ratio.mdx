---
title: Diversification Ratio
summary: How much a fund's own swings shrank once its holdings' correlations are counted, instead of just added up.
---

## What it measures

If a fund's holdings each moved completely independently of one another,
the fund's own volatility would be smaller than any single holding's —
some swings up would cancel out swings down elsewhere in the basket.
This ratio asks how much smaller: the weighted average of each holding's
own volatility, over the volatility the fund actually has once the real
correlations between its holdings are counted in.

A ratio of 1.0 means the holdings move in lockstep — owning all of them
together bought nothing beyond owning one. The further above 1 it
climbs, the more the fund's own ups and downs are cushioned by holdings
that do not all move the same way at the same time. It can never fall
below 1.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{ratio} = \frac{\sum_i w_i \sigma_i}{\sigma_{\text{fund}}}`} />

`σᵢ` is each tracked holding's own annualised volatility and `σ_fund` is
the fund's, both read off the same joint covariance matrix
`services.stats.risk_contribution` builds from the fund's tracked
holdings, priced over the same window the correlation matrix and the
% of ETF column already use. A holding that hasn't traded for the whole
window is left out of this figure rather than narrowing the window to
fit it — see `services/fund_metrics.py`'s own docstring for why.

## How it reads

A low ratio (close to 1) says the fund's diversification is mostly
nominal — many names, but they tend to rise and fall together, often
because they share a sector or a macro exposure. A higher ratio says the
fund's breadth is actually doing work: its own swings are meaningfully
gentler than its individual holdings' swings would suggest on their own.

## Caveats

This is a property of the fund's **tracked** holdings only — those
weighing at least 1% of the fund (see Tracked Weight Coverage). A fund
whose weight sits mostly outside that threshold is describing less of
itself than the ratio implies. It also says nothing about which holdings
are driving the fund's risk — Top 5 Variance Share is the figure for
that.
