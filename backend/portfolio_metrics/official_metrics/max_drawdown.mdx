title: Max Drawdown
summary: The deepest fall from a prior peak the portfolio experienced in the window.
---

## What it measures

How far the portfolio fell from its own high-water mark at the worst
point in the window, and when the peak and the trough it fell to
happened. Total return and CAGR describe where a run ended up; this is
the number that describes what it felt like to be holding it on the worst
day.

## How it is computed

Measured on the flow-free unit value — the only series it makes sense to
watch for a fall, since a raw total can rise purely because money was
paid in on a day the market did nothing:

<Formula tex={String.raw`\text{drawdown}_t = \frac{\text{unitValue}_t - \max_{s \le t}(\text{unitValue}_s)}{\max_{s \le t}(\text{unitValue}_s)}`} />

The reported figure is the most negative value this series reaches,
alongside the date of the peak it fell from and the date of the trough it
reached — a `{value, peakDate, troughDate}` object, not a bare percentage.

## How it reads

A deeper number is a rougher ride, full stop — there is no "good"
drawdown. Read the dates alongside the value: a −20% recovered in six
weeks and a −20% still underwater a year later are very different
experiences this figure alone cannot distinguish; that distinction is
what Time Under Water and the Pain Index, filed separately, exist to add.

## Caveats

0% is a real, common answer — it means the portfolio never fell below a
prior peak in this window, not that the figure could not be computed.
Measured on the total the portfolio actually held, never on any single
holding in isolation: one position can fall much further than the whole
basket without the portfolio itself drawing down nearly as much.
