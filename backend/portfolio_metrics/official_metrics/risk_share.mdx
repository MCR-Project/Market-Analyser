title: Risk Share
summary: Each holding's own share of the basket's total variance, as a percentage that sums to 100.
---

## What it measures

A holding's dollar weight says how much of the basket's *money* it is.
Risk Share says how much of the basket's *risk* it is — and the two can
be very different. A small, volatile position that moves against the
rest of the basket can carry a large share of the risk on a small share
of the money; a large position that moves opposite everything else can
carry less risk than its weight would suggest, occasionally even a
negative share.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`s_i = \frac{w_i \cdot \operatorname{Cov}(r_i, r_p)}{\operatorname{Var}(r_p)} \times 100`} />

The Euler decomposition of the basket's total variance: each holding's
weight times its covariance with the basket's own return, over the
basket's total variance — the same arithmetic `services.fund_metrics.py`
already applies to a fund's tracked holdings, applied here to a
simulated basket instead. Every holding's share is read off one shared
joint covariance matrix, the same one Average Correlation and Effective
Bets are built from.

## How it reads

The shares sum to 100% across the basket (within rounding). A holding
whose share is larger than its weight is contributing more risk than its
size alone would suggest; smaller says the opposite. A negative share is
real, not an error — it means that holding's own moves have, on net,
offset the rest of the basket's rather than added to it.

## Caveats

<Note>
A share can come out above 100% or below 0%. That is not an error: a
holding whose own moves reliably offset the rest of the basket's carries
negative risk, and the holdings that remain must then account for more
than the whole between them. Two holdings that move perfectly opposite
each other are the clearest case — one's share lands over 100%, the
other's under 0%, and the two still sum to 100.
</Note>

This is not shown as a tile — a per-holding breakdown does not fit a
single number, and there is no place on the summary that reads a value
per holding today. It still has this documentation and a real worked
example, the same "no tile, still documented" decision the dividend
figures made.

Null (for every holding at once) under the same conditions Average
Correlation and Effective Bets are: fewer than two holdings with a
complete price history over the window, or no measurable variance at all
once aligned. Never null for a basket of one holding, which trivially
carries 100% of its own risk.
