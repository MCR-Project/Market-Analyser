---
title: Effective Bets
summary: The number of equally weighted holdings that would concentrate risk the way this basket's actual risk contributions do.
---

## What it measures

A ten-holding basket where one position carries 90% of the risk does not
behave like ten bets — it behaves like one, with nine afterthoughts.
Effective Bets answers "how many genuinely independent bets is this
basket actually making", read off each holding's own share of the
basket's *risk* rather than off its dollar weight.

## How it is computed

<Formula tex={String.raw`N_{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_i p_i^2}, \quad p_i = \frac{|s_i|}{\sum_j |s_j|}`} />

The inverse Herfindahl-Hirschman index of `riskShare`'s own magnitudes —
the same concentration formula `services.stats.herfindahl`/`effective_n`
already apply to a set of weights, applied here to each holding's own
share of the basket's variance instead. A basket whose risk shares are
equal (`1/n` each) scores `n`; one where a single holding carries all of
it scores `1`, however many holdings sit beside it.

## How it reads

Between 1 and the holding count, always — never below 1 (all the risk in
one holding) and never above the number of holdings (every holding
contributing its risk independently). A basket of one holding is always
exactly 1: one holding is one bet, by definition, regardless of how much
price history is behind it.

A number well short of the holding count says the basket's diversification
is mostly nominal — many names, but a handful of them are doing almost
all of the moving.

## Caveats

<Note>
Computed from each holding's risk share by *magnitude*, not its signed
value. Risk Share can go negative for a holding that reliably offsets
the rest of the basket, and the inverse-Herfindahl formula above only
stays between 1 and the holding count when every share it is fed is
non-negative — using the signed shares directly would let one hedging
holding push this figure outside that range.
</Note>

This is a property of *risk*, not of dollar weight — a basket that looks
evenly split on the holdings table can still report a low Effective Bets
if a few of those equal-weighted positions happen to be far more volatile
or far more correlated with the rest than the others. Riding shotgun with
Average Correlation and Risk Share gives the fuller picture: this number
says how concentrated, those two say why.
